What's the purpose of this?

This website is sort of like a love letter to older, mostly-physical or physical-only video games. In 2020, the world made a massive shift in the gaming industry due to Covid-19 forcing everyone inside their homes for months on end. Since nobody could leave their homes, more and more people began to busy themselves with video games, and the market began to shift to focus primarily on digital sales, becoming extremely convienent during a global pandemic.

Now that the Covid pandemic has ceased, you would think that things would go back to the way they used to be! But nope, corporations remain as greedy as ever, and the push to abandon physical media has only persisted. This website exsists as a love letter to these games, and I am creating this with the hopes that more people will make an active attempt to purchase more physical gaming media, literally any time it is possible!

This site will have done it's job if you leave with the desire to pick physical over digital purchase options! I'm not the kind of person that believes that older is always better, but this shift towards digital-only doesn't benefit the people it's made for, and instead supports the corporations who only want more money in their pockets at the end of the day.

So what's the problem with digital media?

Absolutely nothing! Well, sort of. Video games themselves are considered digital media, as is anything else you view through a screen. Something being digital isn't an issue, moreso how said digital media is distributed. In the 2010's and prior, it was more common to go to a physical game store or order a disc or cartridge online to purchase new titles.

You could use an "e-shop" to buy a game digitally and have it directly installed on your console. This was a less popular way of buying these games, since you technically didn't own the game physically. If your console broke, or otherwise got bricked, the game would be wiped with it and you would no longer own it once the device was replaced.

Today, most game companies fail to sell more than 40% of physical game copies in comparison to their digital counterparts. What's more upsetting is that the majority of people really are not fond of this new market standard, as it goes against gamer's desires!

How did we get to this point?

These sorts of changes just tend to progress naturally over time for the most part, but with video games in particular? I've found it mostly comes down to game publishers becoming very greedy.

As you've likely gathered, making the switch to primarily selling digital releases rather than continuing to mainly sell physical discs and cartrigdes is MUCH cheaper. So now not only is it cheaper for the company, but it effectively makes it more expensive for consumers.

As I briefly mentioned, when you purchase a digital game you no longer "own" it in the same way you used to own a game when you would go to the store and bring it home. If your console breaks and it remains dysfunctional, you can never remove the game from the system and play it on a new one. It is effectively gone. Owning a game fully digitally also means you can no longer share it with family members unless if you want them to use your own system, forcing households to buy more than one copy of these games.

Ultimately, this new standard currently only exists to put more money in the pockets of these business owners.
